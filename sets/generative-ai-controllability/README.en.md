# The Roadmap of Generative AI

The roadmap of generative AI: use cases and applications. This is a translation of
[the original Chinese diagrams](./README.md).

This diagram set was drawn in June 2023, as a forward-looking chart based on the capabilities of
LLMs and generative AI models at the time. Each diagram's description below covers its core logic,
along with a few supplementary notes or corrections drawn from what actually happened between 2024
and 2026.

## Figure 1, Evolution of Controllability

![Controllability: The Evolution of Generative AI](./diagrams/01-controllability-evolution.en.png)

Drawn in June 2023, this diagram tries to capture the evolution of "controllability" — the core dimension along which generative AI progresses. It moves from fully uncontrollable generation, through direction-level and structural/local-logic controllability, to what was then a newly emerging "controllable chain of thought," and on toward more distant complex reasoning and rule/principle-level controllability. The diagram marks its "current state" (2023) between controllable chain-of-thought and controllable complex reasoning, leaving planning/decision-making, memory/learning, advanced reasoning, and eventually self-evolution and systems thinking as "future development."

Looking back from 2024-2026, the direction held up, but the pace outran the original expectations: models with long reasoning chains, agents that can plan and execute autonomously, and loop-engineering practices that self-correct against verifiable rewards have turned "controllable complex reasoning" into an everyday capability, with some frontier systems already brushing up against rule/principle-level controllability.

## Figure 2, Application Trends by Controllability

![Controllability: Application Trends](./diagrams/02-application-trends.en.png)

Building on the controllability axis from Figure 1, this chart maps typical application directions and generatable content onto each controllability level — from early assisted design and assisted creation, through Copilot-mode efficiency tools and business-process automation, to the predicted "AI as OS," robotics and autonomous driving, and self-iterating software/hardware systems.

Three years on, the Copilot model and "AI-assisted programming" have become mainstream engineering practice, and early forms of "AI as OS" are visible in computer-use agents and OS-level AI assistants. What was labeled "future development" back then — self-iterating software/hardware system design — is precisely the capability described by "loop engineering" and the "AGI era" in this repository's other diagram set, [2026 - The Critical Path to Enterprise AI Capability](../enterprise-ai-critical-path/README.en.md). In that sense, this 2023 forecast and the 2026 roadmap turn out to trace the same logical arc.

## Figure 3, Typical AI Applications

![Controllability: Application Categories and Cases](./diagrams/03-application-categories.en.png)

From a mid-2023 vantage point, this diagram lists concrete products and startups for each stage of the controllability curve — from content-creation tools like Dall·E 2 and Midjourney, to early agent frameworks like LangChain and AutoGPT, to early "AI as OS" prototypes like ChatGPT Plugins. These names are a snapshot of the generative-AI startup landscape at that moment, not a durable list.

More than three years later, many of these products have been consolidated, superseded, or absorbed elsewhere: plugin ecosystems for connecting tools have given way to more standardized tool-calling protocols, and early experimental autonomous-agent frameworks have matured into sturdier engineering paradigms (see the discussion of "harness engineering" and "loop engineering" in [2026 - The Critical Path to Enterprise AI Capability](../enterprise-ai-critical-path/README.en.md)). When reading this diagram, it's more useful to focus on the mapping between controllability stage and application logic than on the specific product names.

## Figure 4, Evolution of Multimodal AI Applications

![Controllability: Multimodal Use Cases](./diagrams/04-multimodal-capabilities.en.png)

Focused on multimodal AI, this diagram lays out five threads along the controllability axis — content platforms, cross-modal perception and interaction, dynamic content generation, static/3D content generation, and multimodal infrastructure — and predicts future directions such as "plot-controllable long-form video generation," "professional-grade music composition," and AI that can autonomously present and explain content.

Multimodal generation progress from 2024 to 2026 has validated, and in some cases outpaced, this forecast: newer video generation models can already produce coherent, longer-duration clips with controllable plot, and music and voice generation are closing in on professional production quality. Lagging behind is fully automated, industrial-grade 3D scene reconstruction and real-time virtual-world construction — that thread is still moving from "parameterized 3D generation" toward "high-quality 3D scene generation," and hasn't yet fully caught up with what the diagram envisioned as "future development."
